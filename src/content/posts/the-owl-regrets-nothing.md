<p class="kz-lead">A week before he died, Miyamoto Musashi wrote twenty-one lines and handed them to a student. No title ceremony. No explanation. The Dokkōdō — the path of walking alone.</p>
<p>Most self-improvement is furniture arranged for other people's visits. The Dokkōdō clears the room. Accept everything as it stands. Do not regret what you have done. Do not seek to possess goods or fame. Be detached from desire your whole life. The owl nods from its perch — this is already how it lives: it takes the night as it arrives, eats when there is prey, sits when there is none, and wastes no second on the version of the hunt it would have liked better.</p>

<blockquote>
  "Accept everything just as it is. Do not regret what you have done."
  <span class="kz-who">— miyamoto musashi · dokkōdō, precept ii &amp; viii</span>
</blockquote>

<p>Read at low resolution, it sounds like renunciation. Read properly, it is an operating manual for intensity: a man carried two swords and never once skirmished gossip; he put the entire budget of his attention into one recurring, boring decision — the cut, today, done properly. Stoicism and Musashi agree with the owl on the core loop: control what is inside the frame, drop everything outside it, and never turn the frame into a complaint.</p>
<p>So run your week as a Dokkōdō experiment, not as a mood: one goal, noroadcast, no regret on the worn path. In the dark, the red readout never apologizes — <span class="kz-mono" style="color:var(--red); text-shadow:0 0 8px var(--glow)">33:27</span> — it just keeps reporting the same truth until the night agrees.</p>
